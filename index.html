Riverside Collective

A worker-owned coffee shop and ice cream bar in South Jackson, Mississippi, run by the students and shaped by our neighbors.

I started working on Riverside Collective during my planning periods at Blackburn Middle School. I saw how my students struggled to understand why their education mattered and the ways systemic disinvestment shaped their lives. Riverside is a worker-owned coffeehouse and student entrepreneurship program in South Jackson designed to show middle and high schoolers how their education can economically transform South Jackson.

I spent my time building relationships with business leaders, foundations, local government, community groups, and parents to communicate our vision for cooperative economics. I secured a donated building and raised over $200,000 in philanthropic capital. I worked with contractors to fix our plumbing, redo our internal wiring, and install an HVAC system. I learned how to use a chainsaw and cut down overgrown bushes. When I couldn't be on-site, my teammates stepped up. Together, we coordinated transportation for kids, painted a mural, moved furniture, built cabinets, and designed a space that reflected pride in South Jackson. My students learned how to make lattes, develop marketing plans, and make decisions as a team. We opened in July of 2025.

If you mix hard work with a group of people who care, transformation is inevitable. Riverside is our proof.

Great Plains Institute

I served as the inaugural environmental justice fellow at the Great Plains Institute. I worked on carbon management policy and communication, municipal energy resilience planning, and community engagement.

State roadmaps in Texas and Colorado

I met with stakeholders in Texas and Colorado to write environmental justice language into their state carbon management roadmaps. I balanced the health and safety concerns of grassroots organizations with the incentives of developers and the economic priorities of state legislators.

The Civic Toolkit

I helped launch the Civic Toolkit, a guide for developers on working with communities.

The civic toolkit is designed for communities, developers, decision makers interested in carbon management technologies. The toolkit has educational resources, strategies for effective communication, and best practices to engage with communities.

mississippi

A path to a just transition

Communities across America are skeptical of climate technologies. We need clean energy projects to stop climate change, and we need communities to be bought in. My work in Mississippi was to understand people's concerns about climate. Could we find a shared framework that protected communities and advanced decarbonization?

stop 1 · listening

Seventeen conversations

I travelled across the state and spoke to 17 leaders, one-on-one, to understand their views on climate. I learned that people cared about climate, but didn't understand the science or how to mitigate climate change's impacts.

stop 2 · convening

The Climate Learning Convening

In response, I organized the Climate Learning Convening where I convened more than 20 organizations, from food equity advocates to policymakers. I brought in trusted experts to explain the difference between baseload and intermittent power, ran workshops on greenhouse gas emissions, and facilitated roundtables where we imagined what Mississippi's Clean Energy Economy could look like.

destination · a just transition

Mississippi's first Just Transition Plan

The result was the state's first Just Transition Plan. Our plan endorsed a wide range of clean energy technologies as long as they met conditions of safety, transparency, and investment. For carbon management technologies, this is a first-of-a-kind achievement.

My work is a model for the developers who care about deploying projects the right way. Strong relationships, community investment, and a commitment to transparency are crucial to getting good projects built.
